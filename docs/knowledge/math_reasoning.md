# Mathematical Reasoning

## Equations and substitution

For an equation with an unknown variable, first state the allowed domain and isolate the unknown symbolically. Substitute known values only after the relationship is clear. Verify the result by substitution into the original equation, not merely by repeating the same algebra in a different format.

## Arithmetic, units, and dimensions

Use exact arithmetic when the inputs and required answer are exact. Carry units through every calculation and reject additions or comparisons with incompatible dimensions. Estimate the order of magnitude before accepting a numeric result; a unit check and an independent recomputation catch different classes of error.

## Boundaries and optimization

An optimum can occur at an interior stationary point or at a boundary. Enumerate domain boundaries, discontinuities, and integer restrictions before ranking candidates. Test boundary cases explicitly and distinguish a local optimum from the global result required by the problem.

## Probability and base rates

Probability claims require a clearly defined event, reference class, and conditioning information. Start from the base rate, update with evidence whose likelihood is understood, and avoid treating correlation as independence. Report uncertainty when sample size or selection mechanisms are unknown.
