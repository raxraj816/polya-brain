# General Problem-Solving Playbooks

## Clarify the real goal

Restate the problem as a decision or outcome and define what would count as solved. Separate user-owned preferences from discoverable facts. When an ambiguity changes the solution, ask one focused clarifying question instead of inventing the missing requirement.

## Decompose by dependencies

Break a broad problem into subproblems whose outputs can be observed or verified. Identify blockers, information dependencies, reversible actions, and decision points. Solve the highest-information or highest-risk dependency first rather than following an arbitrary checklist.

## Generate and compare hypotheses

Produce multiple mechanism-level explanations or solution paths before ranking them. For each candidate, state expected signals, evidence against, constraints, feasibility, falsification criteria, and verification cost. Analogy may suggest a candidate, but first principles must explain and evidence must decide.

## Verify, reflect, and stop honestly

Try to disprove the proposed conclusion with checks tied to success criteria. Record contradictions and open questions. After verification, reflect on what worked, what failed, and what may transfer; if verification cannot close the loop, stop with insufficient evidence rather than forcing an answer.
