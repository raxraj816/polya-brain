# Technical Research

## Prefer primary sources

For product behavior, standards, APIs, or scientific claims, search for official documentation, specifications, source code, or the original paper. A secondary summary can help discover vocabulary, but it should not silently replace the primary source that actually supports the claim.

## Corroborate independently

Verify important factual claims with multiple independent sources. Syndicated copies and articles repeating one announcement are not independent evidence. Record what each source directly establishes and separate background context from support or contradiction of the claim under investigation.

## Check freshness and applicability

Record publication date, product version, jurisdiction, population, and other applicability boundaries. Current questions should not rely on stale documentation when behavior may have changed. A source can be reliable yet irrelevant to the version or case being solved.

## Resolve contradictions

When sources conflict, do not average them into false confidence. Compare definitions, dates, methods, incentives, and primary evidence. State the contradiction explicitly, seek a discriminating source or test, and conclude insufficient evidence when the disagreement cannot be resolved.
